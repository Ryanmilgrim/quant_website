Black-Scholes Option Pricing

Price European calls and puts with clear inputs and deterministic math.

Derivatives desks use Black-Scholes as a baseline valuation for European options. The model's assumptions — constant volatility, no dividends, continuous trading — make it a useful benchmark even when real-world pricing departs from it.

Model Overview ▼
Notation

\( S \) — spot price of the underlying asset.
\( K \) — strike price of the option.
\( T \) — time to expiry in years.
\( r \) — continuously compounded risk-free interest rate.
\( \sigma \) — annualized volatility of the underlying's log returns.
\( N(\cdot) \) — cumulative distribution function of the standard normal distribution.

Call and Put Prices

The Black-Scholes model gives closed-form prices for European options:

\[ C = S\,N(d_1) - K\,e^{-rT}\,N(d_2) \]
\[ P = K\,e^{-rT}\,N(-d_2) - S\,N(-d_1) \]

where

\[ d_1 = \frac{\ln(S/K) + \left(r + \tfrac{\sigma^2}{2}\right)T}{\sigma\sqrt{T}}, \qquad d_2 = d_1 - \sigma\sqrt{T} \]
Key Assumptions

The underlying asset follows geometric Brownian motion with log-normal price dynamics.

Volatility \( \sigma \) is constant over the life of the option.

The underlying pays no dividends during the option's life.

Trading is continuous and frictionless (no transaction costs or market impact).

The option can only be exercised at expiry (European exercise only).

Put-Call Parity
\[ C - P = S - K\,e^{-rT} \]

Put-call parity links the prices of a European call and put with the same strike and expiry. Given any three of \( C \), \( P \), \( S \), and \( K\,e^{-rT} \), the fourth is determined. A violation of this relationship implies an arbitrage opportunity (absent transaction costs).
