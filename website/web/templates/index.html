The Fundamental Law

The analytical framework behind this site follows Grinold & Kahn’s Fundamental Law of Active Management:

\[\text{IR} \approx \text{IC} \times \sqrt{\text{BR}}\]

IC (Information Coefficient) — correlation between forecasted and realized returns. Better signals produce a higher IC. Adaptive risk models, time-varying betas, and regime detection are all designed to improve forecast quality.

BR (Breadth) — the number of independent bets per year. Systematic, repeatable analysis across many assets and time periods increases breadth. A single view on large-cap vs small-cap can inform many allocation decisions through correlation.

IR (Information Ratio) — risk-adjusted active return. The measure of skill. Every tool on this site is built to improve one or both sides of the equation.

Framework: Richard C. Grinold & Ronald N. Kahn, Active Portfolio Management (1999).

Design principle

Interpretability

Every model on this site is designed to be understood, not just run. Factor decompositions show which risks matter. Regime probabilities have economic meaning. Style weights are investable and sum to 100%.

No black-box algorithms — every output can be traced back to its inputs and assumptions.

Data: Fama-French Data Library (1926–present) and Federal Reserve Economic Data (FRED). All analysis is ex-post on historical data.
